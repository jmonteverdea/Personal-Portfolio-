About

What I do.

I run clean energy programs across Canada and Mexico. Government, private sector, and conservation authorities. The pattern is the same every time. Take dense technical material, build the system that lets people act on it, then measure what gets delivered.

In Mexico I authored the country's first national household energy efficiency guide for extreme-heat climates. Trained 150+ municipal inspectors and households. Then scaled a residential solar program to 200+ installations and 3 MW, before managing USD 1 to 2 million enterprise deployments through a 25-partner network.

In Canada I scoped decarbonization and workforce roadmaps for 30 active cleantech and natural-resource clients at Granted Consulting and coordinated CAD 200K+ in approved funding. I also led a low-carbon technology assessment for the Toronto and Region Conservation Authority, evaluating seven pathways and shortlisting heat pump technology, which was adopted into TRCA's decarbonization planning.

What I believe.

Most clean energy programs fail at delivery, not at policy. The plans are usually fine. The retrofits don't ship because no one funded the training, the partner network, or the inspector capacity. The work I take on lives in that gap.

What I'm looking for.

Full-time Program Manager roles in energy, sustainability, or climate. Regional or federal government, mission-driven consultancies, or climate startups scaling delivery. Bilingual EN/ES. Open across North America. Available now.

02 / 04

Bright Inc.

Renewable Energy Advisor → Regional Manager, Residential Solar → Partner Manager, Enterprise Solar

July 2020 to August 2024. Mexico.

3 MW across 200+ solar projects. Three promotions in four years.

Challenge

Bright needed to scale residential solar across Mexico and move upmarket into enterprise. The bottleneck was execution, not demand. Partner quality varied, on-time completion was inconsistent, and corporate accounts needed a delivery system that did not exist yet. At volume, every site also meant utility interconnection, permitting, and environmental compliance under inconsistent local procedures. Quality had to hold across hundreds of installations or the program would lose the trust of clients and utilities.

Approach

Built KPI-based operating playbooks and standardized the path from intake to installation. Set the same documentation and approval checkpoints for every project. Built durable relationships with utility counterparts to keep interconnection timelines predictable, and managed environmental compliance at the site level. Ran 500+ technical consultations to turn design intent into deliverable systems. Scaled and managed a 25-partner delivery network with quality control, then launched a pilot enterprise solar program and grew it through commissioning of USD 1 to 2 million projects.

03 / 04

CEDES. Comisión de Ecología y Desarrollo Sustentable

Project Director, Energy Efficiency

July 2019 to June 2020. Mexico.

150+ inspectors and households trained. Mexico's first national guide.

Challenge

Mexico had no national residential energy efficiency standard adapted to extreme-heat regions. Municipal inspectors and households in hot climates were operating without practical, climate-specific guidance. Existing references were not localized, not in Spanish at the level required, and not connected to a training pathway.

Approach

Government-funded research program. Authored the country's first national energy efficiency guide for extreme-heat climates. Built cost-benefit analyses for insulation and HVAC bundles. Designed and delivered a training curriculum aligned to ASHRAE 90.1 equivalent standards.

Outcome

150+trained on ASHRAE 90.1 eq.
1stnational guide of its kind
2020published

04 / 04

Toronto and Region Conservation Authority

Project Lead, Newcomer Youth Green Economy Project

October 2024 to March 2025. Toronto, ON. Remote.

7 3 low-carbon pathways evaluated, three shortlisted

Challenge

TRCA needed an evidence-based shortlist of low-carbon technology pathways for newcomer-youth green economy programming, aligned to Canada's 2030 emissions targets. The constraints: a 9-person research team, a fixed timeline, and outputs that internal decision-makers could act on without rework.

Approach

Built a structured work plan with explicit quality assurance gates. Evaluated seven low-carbon technology pathways against cost, risk, and feasibility criteria. Synthesized findings into technical summaries and briefing materials that mapped directly to internal decision points.

Outcome
